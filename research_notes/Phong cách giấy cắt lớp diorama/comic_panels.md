# Comic-Panel Storytelling in Games: Layout, Transitions, Motion-Comic Animation, Pacing (for a ~6-beat paper-craft intro + win/lose endings)

Scope note: about 15 searches and fetches. Several requested examples (Ruined King, Sunset Overdrive, Borderlands, Bandle Tale, Hades, Slay the Princess, Marvel's Spider-Man intros, Unpacking) returned no usable sources on their comic-panel presentation. They are listed under Gaps rather than described from memory. Many comics-craft sources are blogs or tutorial sites, so treat their numbers as conventions, not standards.

## 1. Panel layout grammar (panels per page, reading order, size/emphasis, splash, gutters, borders, broken frames)

### Takeaway
Print convention is about 4-7 panels per page. Fewer, bigger panels slow the reader and add weight. Splash (full-page) panels are kept for the rare peak moment. Gutters are usually uniform, so changing gutter width or removing it carries meaning (time passing, speed, a big moment). For a game intro, 2-4 panels per screen with one dominant "hero" panel per beat fits these conventions best.

### Cited Findings
- Makingcomics says "five to seven panels per page is a good balance." — [MakingComics: Flow page part 1](https://www.makingcomics.com/2014/03/17/flow-page-part-1/)
- Another guide says most superhero comics use 4-6 panels per page. Fast action pages may use 2-3 large panels, and conversation pages may go to 8-9 small ones. Panel count directly affects reading speed: fewer panels read slower. (Low-authority, possibly auto-generated site.) — [Comic Style Hub: superhero panel layout](https://comic-style-hub-l9.pages.dev/superhero-comic-panel-layout-best-practices-comic-panel-layout-techniques)
- A splash page (one image filling the page) is reserved for big reveals, dramatic entrances or emotional peaks, and overuse dulls its impact. Rule of thumb: "Two splash pages in a 22-page comic is generous." (Same low-authority site; advice, not an industry rule.) — [Comic Style Hub](https://comic-style-hub-l9.pages.dev/superhero-comic-panel-layout-best-practices-comic-panel-layout-techniques)
- Clip Studio's Art Rocket tutorial says gutters are traditionally kept the same width so the eye moves smoothly between panels. Changing gutter width can express time and add realism. — [Clip Studio: Pro Artist's Guide to Comic & Manga Layouts, Paneling, Flow](https://clipstudio.net/how-to-draw/archives/160963)
- Typical print gutter is about 0.125-0.25 in (about 0.25 in "industry standard"). Webtoon gutters are 50-200 px, and narrow gutters speed up pacing. Splash pages drop gutters entirely. — [Brainbound: comic gutters guide](https://brainbound.blog/comic-gutters-guide); [GlobalComix: gutters and panel layouts](https://globalcomix.com/news/details/249/creator-tips-and-tricks-8-gutters-and-panel-layouts)
- "Rolling transitions" means dissolving panel borders so images overlap and fold together, giving fluid movement through space and time. This is a borderless or broken-frame technique. — [Sal Good Sam: Transitions](https://salgoodsam.com/mc/transitions/)
- Terminology varies: "splash" may mean any full-page panel, while "full-width panel" or "panorama" means a row-wide panel. — [Hooded Utilitarian: Analyzing Comics 101 (Layout)](https://www.hoodedutilitarian.com/?p=67660)
- Webtoon (vertical-scroll) paneling is its own grammar, using spacing between panels as the main pacing tool. — [Content Curve: Introduction to webtoon paneling](https://contentcurve.substack.com/p/introduction-to-webtoon-paneling)

### Inferences
- A 16:9 screen holds about half the content of a print page. So 2-4 panels per "page" (screen) is the screen equivalent of a 4-7 panel print page, and this matches the motion-comic approach of revealing one panel at a time.
- Suggested mapping for a 6-beat intro: Page 1, the job (3 panels: office establishing, boss, "FIRED" reaction close-up). Page 2, the creditor (2-3 panels ending in a large creditor panel). Page 3, the restaurant reveal as the one splash or near-splash panel, followed by a debt-schedule inset or prop. Endings: one splash panel each (win: the restaurant thriving; lose: the shutters closing), each preceded by 1-2 small panels.
- Reading order: keep a strict left-to-right Z path (rows top to bottom) for a Vietnamese and English audience. Use the reveal order to enforce it, so the reader never has to infer it.
- Gutters in a cozy paper-craft style: a warm off-white or kraft-paper page with medium gutters reads "cozy." A dark page reads noir, as in Max Payne. Use one deliberate exception, such as a zero-gutter or broken frame for the "FIRED!" or creditor shock beat.
- Tilted panels and panels breaking their frame suit agitation (getting fired, the creditor looming). Calm, straight panels suit the restaurant reveal and the win ending.

### Gaps
- No primary source (for example Will Eisner's *Comics and Sequential Art* or McCloud's *Making Comics*) was fetched for panel-count or gutter numbers. The figures above come from blogs and tutorials.
- No source on dark versus white gutter color's effect on mood. The noir and cozy mapping is an inference.

## 2. Panel-to-panel transitions (McCloud's six types) and which suit a short narrative

### Takeaway
McCloud's six types are moment-to-moment, action-to-action, subject-to-subject, scene-to-scene, aspect-to-aspect and non-sequitur. A short 6-beat story should lean on action-to-action and subject-to-subject inside each beat, use scene-to-scene between beats, and save moment-to-moment for one dramatic slow-down. Aspect-to-aspect suits a cozy establishing mood, such as the restaurant's details.

### Cited Findings
- Definitions: moment-to-moment covers tiny time steps ("a blink"), slows action and gets dull if overused. Action-to-action is a sequence of significant actions, the workhorse of superhero comics. Subject-to-subject shifts focus within the same scene, for example between speakers. Scene-to-scene changes location or jumps in time. Aspect-to-aspect wanders around one scene to set mood and invites lingering. Non-sequitur has no overt connection and is rare outside surreal or gag comics. Abel and Madden add a seventh type, "symbolic," for imagined or felt content and flashbacks. — [Sal Good Sam: Transitions](https://salgoodsam.com/mc/transitions/)
- McCloud's third chapter grounds the transitions in "closure": the reader fills in what happens in the gutter. — [Natsume Fusanosuke essay, ejcjs](https://www.japanesestudies.org.uk/ejcjs/vol21/iss2/holt_fukuda.html); [EBSCO research starter on Understanding Comics](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/literature-and-writing/understanding-comics-invisible-art-scott-mccloud)
- Empirical work (Neil Cohn and colleagues) finds that American and European comics mostly use clear action changes, then shifts between characters and locations. Manga makes similar shifts but also moves between aspects of the environment, and uses more subjective, character-viewpoint panels. — [eScholarship paper on cross-cultural panel transitions](https://escholarship.org/content/qt7cx1j5q0/qt7cx1j5q0.pdf?t=op2lql)
- McCloud's emphasis on aspect-to-aspect is especially relevant to Japanese comics. — [Journal of Literary... (Pitt) article](https://jll.pitt.edu/ojs/JLL/article/download/170/526)
- Remedy's Markus Mäki argued that a graphic novel lets the player fill in nuance that would be hard to reach with in-game or prerendered cinematics. This is closure used as a production advantage. — [Max Payne research via search summary of GamesRadar making-of](https://gamesradar.com/making-of-max-payne-20th-anniversary)

### Inferences
- Beat-by-beat suggestion:
  1. Fired: action-to-action (box handed over, then walking out), with one moment-to-moment slow-down on the face.
  2. Walk home or the street: scene-to-scene.
  3. Creditor appears: subject-to-subject (the hero, then the creditor's shadow or hand).
  4. Offer of the restaurant: subject-to-subject dialogue.
  5. The restaurant: aspect-to-aspect (dusty sign, the stove, a cat) to give a cozy feel.
  6. The debt schedule: a close-up "insert" of a paper prop (calendar with day 21/22 circled), which works as a functional UI bridge into gameplay.
- Avoid non-sequitur except as a joke beat.

### Gaps
- McCloud's exact frequency chart (percentages per transition type for American vs Japanese comics) was not retrieved from a primary source. Commonly cited figures (action-to-action most common in Western comics; more aspect-to-aspect in manga) are qualitatively consistent with the Cohn study above, but exact numbers are unverified.

## 3. Revealing and animating panels in games (pop-in, camera across the page, parallax, limited animation, bubbles, SFX lettering)

### Takeaway
Motion comics get their sense of life cheaply. They layer each panel (background, mid, actors, frame, effects) and drift a camera with parallax, use limited animation (move one or two elements, not whole frames), cut between panels, and add lettered SFX. The best game examples keep the panel and page visible, rather than collapsing into a "minimal movie," which critics dislike.

### Cited Findings
- A ModDB motion-comic breakdown lists layers: background texture, background parallax, props and actors, frames, and effects (tint, lens flare, dust). (The page returned 403 on fetch; details are from the search snippet.) — [ModDB: Composition of a motion comic](https://www.moddb.com/features/composition-of-a-motion-comics)
- Limited animation means animating individual elements within a still image instead of redrawing frames, which is why motion looks less smooth. — [Moose 1984 blog: limited animation](https://moose-1984.blogspot.com/2015/04/limited-animation.html); [Wikipedia: Motion comic](https://www.Wikipedia.com/wiki/Motion_comic)
- A JOLT trailer case study: parallax camera movement combined with jump cuts "creates the illusion of movement and motion, without actually requiring expensive and time consuming character animation." — [Jumbla: JOLT](https://jumbla.com/work/jolt)
- Guides suggest holding the panel still and animating only one or two things (rain, a flickering lamp, a single gesture) plus SFX. — [Creative Bloq: motion comics with Cartoon Animator 5](https://creativebloq.com/features/how-to-create-motion-comics-cartoon-animator)
- Critique (USC "Timeframing"): motion comics like *Watchmen* (2008) "effectively turn the comic into a minimally animated movie" and remove the panel's "temporal map." Art not designed to move gets pushed past its limits, and fan reception is polarized. Madefire's "motion books" keep panels visible while adding limited animation. *Ultimate Spider-Man* (2005) cutscenes mixed 2D, 3D and full-motion animation inside comic panels. — [USC Scalar: Watchmen motion comic](https://scalar.usc.edu/works/timeframing/watchmen-motion-comic); [USC Scalar: Madefire motion books](https://scalar.usc.edu/works/timeframing/madefires-motion-books)
- Konami's *Metal Gear Solid: Portable Ops* and *Peace Walker* used limited-animation, motion-comic cutscenes with art by Ashley Wood. — [Giant Bomb: Motion Comic concept](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Concepts/Motion_Comic)
- Gravity Rush: story cutscenes are comic pages the player scrolls through via the touchscreen. Tilting the Vita changes perspective because the frame and panel layers move independently, a 2.5D parallax effect. Toyama said experiments with animating graphics inside the panels did not make the final game. One reviewer called the tilt "a neat gimmick but a gimmick nonetheless." Influence: Moebius and Franco-Belgian comics. — [Gematsu interview with Keiichiro Toyama](https://gematsu.com/2012/04/interview-gravity-rushs-keiichiro-toyama); [Pocket Gamer hands-on](https://www.pocketgamer.com/gravity-rush/hands-on-with-gravity-rush-on-ps-vita); [MoeGamer: Gravity Rush and the Franco-Belgian tradition](https://moegamer.net/2017/06/08/gravity-rush-the-franco-belgian-tradition-via-japan/); [God is a Geek review](https://godisageek.com/2012/05/gravity-rush-review/)
- Max Payne: interstitial story uses graphic-novel panels with voice-over instead of animated cutscenes, mainly for budget reasons. Panels were staff, friends and family photographed in costume and run through Photoshop watercolor-style filters, because hand-painting was "way too slow." Max Payne 2 kept panels between chapters but added traditional cutscenes once there was budget. — [Game Informer: Making Max Payne](https://www.gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2016/03/27/making-max-payne-how-hong-kong-kung-fu-and-family-photo-shoots-built-a-noir-thriller.aspx); [Film Stories: Scott Miller on Max Payne](https://filmstories.co.uk/features/max-payne-producer-scott-miller-on-the-making-of-a-classic/); [GamesRadar 20th anniversary](https://gamesradar.com/making-of-max-payne-20th-anniversary)
- Comix Zone (Sega Technical Institute, 1995; concept by Peter Morawiec): gameplay takes place inside comic panels. The player jumps panel to panel and sometimes chooses a path, and knocking enemies into a panel border bursts it open. The hero, Sketch, tears through the paper. Critics praised the presentation more than the level design. — [Wikipedia: Comix Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comix_Zone); [Retroware: Comix Zone](https://articles.retroware.com/2020/11/18/comix-zone-a-farewell-to-the-genesis/)
- Framed (Loveshack, 2014): the player rearranges and rotates comic panels to change the order of events, and the game "adapts to any order the panels are placed in." Many "failure" panels add comedy. All animation was hand-made by Stuart Lloyd. Levels were prototyped with paper cut-outs and stick figures, and panel rotation was tuned through playtesting. — [Game Developer: Road to the IGF, Framed](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/road-to-the-igf-loveshack-s-i-framed-i-); [PC Gamer: How Framed makes great chases](https://www.pcgamer.com/how-framed-brings-everything-together-to-make-great-chases-and-escapes)
- Spider-Verse films: Ben-Day dots and halftone as background texture so the frame looks printed. Split screens with bold black lines mimic panels. Some scenes are built as panel sequences that characters comment on. Onomatopoeia and kinetic typography ("Thwip," "Bang") appear on screen, and caption boxes stand in for voice-over. — [GameSpot: why Spider-Verse's comic-book animation works](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/why-spider-man-into-the-spider-verses-comic-book-a/1100-6463955/); [No Film School: comic panels in Spider-Verse](https://nofilmschool.com/comic-panels-in-spiderverse-movies); [Creative Bloq: 7 hidden design secrets](https://www.creativebloq.com/advice/7-hidden-design-secrets-from-into-the-spider-verse); [Little White Lies](https://lwlies.com/article/comic-book-film-adaptations-visual-style)
- Ruined King: Joe Madureira approaches scenes by asking "what is the comic book version of this character or this scene." No source confirmed comic-panel cutscenes specifically. — [Epic Games Store: How Ruined King came to life](https://store.epicgames.com/news/how-ruined-king-came-to-life-as-a-tactical-league-of-legends-rpg)

### Inferences (practical recipe; timings are design suggestions, not sourced values)
- Per-panel structure:
  1. The panel enters (paper slides in about 0.25-0.4 s with ease-out and a small overshoot or settle, or drops onto the page with a tiny bounce and shadow).
  2. The camera eases from the previous panel to this one (about 0.5-0.8 s).
  3. Hold, with a slow parallax drift of 2-5% over the hold.
  4. Bubbles pop in sequentially (scale 0.8 to 1.0 in about 0.15 s), one bubble per click or auto-timed.
  5. The SFX word punches in with scale and rotation on the action frame.
- "Camera across a page then zoom in": open each page zoomed out so the whole page (with blank slots) is visible for about 1 s, then fly to panel 1. At the end of the page, pull back out to show the completed page (a satisfying "page done" moment, like Gravity Rush's scrolled pages), then turn the page or slide in a new sheet.
- Limited animation in panels: per the Creative Bloq and JOLT findings, animate only 1-2 elements per panel. Examples: steam from the restaurant pot, the boss's finger wag, the creditor's cigarette smoke, a blinking eye. Idle loops of 2-4 frames on "twos/threes" (stepped, 8-12 fps) fit the stop-motion look, while the camera and parallax stay smooth. That split hides low frame counts.
- Keep the panel and page visible (avoid the "Watchmen motion comic" collapse), because the frame is the style.
- Endings: reuse the same page/desk system. Win: warm light, the splash panel lands with confetti or paper bunting, and the debt paper gets a red "PAID" stamp SFX. Lose: a desaturated page, the panel slides in slowly with no bounce, and the final panel is the shop's paper shutter coming down, with a quiet SFX ("creak," "click").

### Gaps
- No GDC talk or developer write-up with measured pop-in or camera timings for in-game comic reveals was found. All timing numbers above are inferences.
- The requested examples Sunset Overdrive, Borderlands (intro), Hades, Slay the Princess, Bandle Tale, Unpacking and Marvel's Spider-Man comic intros: searches returned nothing about their panel presentation. Note that Hades, Slay the Princess and Unpacking are not known as comic-panel games and may be off-target. Bandle Tale is papercraft-styled, but no source on its cutscenes was found.

## 4. Pacing, advance model, skip, readability at 1080p, accessibility

### Takeaway
Time text by readable characters per second (about 15-18 CPS, or 160-180 WPM). Prefer click-to-advance per panel or bubble, with optional auto-play. Always allow pause and hold-to-skip. At 1080p, body text should be at least 26 px tall on console and 18 px on PC (Xbox XAG 101), scalable to 200%.

### Cited Findings
- BBC subtitle guidelines recommend 160-180 WPM and a 37-character line. About 180 WPM is roughly 15-18 characters per second. A common rule of thumb keeps a full two-line subtitle on screen for about 6 s. — [Subtitle Toolset: subtitle reading speed](https://subtitle-toolset.pages.dev/guides/subtitle-reading-speed/); [Graffiti Studio: subtitle reading-speed psychology](https://graffitistudio.bg/subtitle-reading-speed-psychology/)
- Adults read about 200-250 WPM in ideal conditions, but subtitle and caption reading happens under divided attention (watching images too), so it should be slower. Comprehension declines as speed increases. — [Graffiti Studio](https://graffitistudio.bg/subtitle-reading-speed-psychology/); [PMC study on subtitle speed](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6007935)
- For animated-in text, count reading time from the stable hold after the entrance finishes, not from layer start. — [Open Design: on-screen text duration](https://open-design.ai/blog/on-screen-text-duration/)
- Game Accessibility Guidelines say to cut subtitles and captions to a WPM appropriate for the target age group. — [Game Accessibility Guidelines: subtitle WPM](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/ensure-that-subtitles-captions-are-cut-down-to-and-presented-at-an-appropriate-words-per-minute-for-the-target-age-group/)
- Xbox Accessibility Guideline 101 sets minimum body text height (lowest descender to highest ascender) at 26 px for console and 18 px for PC at 1080p. Text should resize to 200% without losing content. Third-party sites cite 28 px, which is unverified against Microsoft. — [Xbox XAG 101: Text display](https://devdocs.xbox.com/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/101)
- XAG 116 (time limits) gives an example of a "Hold to skip" prompt alongside an autoplay-cutscenes option. XAG 108: the player can pause at will. — [Xbox XAG 116: Time limits](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/116)
- Game Accessibility Guidelines: offer a means to bypass non-core gameplay elements, such as an in-game skip. — [Game Accessibility Guidelines](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/?p=21)

### Inferences
- Default timing per bubble: max(1.5 s, characters / 15) after the bubble has settled. A wordless panel holds 1.5-2.5 s, and a splash panel 3-4 s. A 6-beat intro of about 14-18 panels then runs roughly 60-90 s. That is short enough not to need chapter skips, but still provide hold-to-skip (about 1 s hold, with a radial fill) for the whole sequence.
- Advance model: click or tap or confirm advances to the next bubble or panel, and pressing it mid-animation completes the current animation instantly ("hurry, then advance"). Offer an "Auto" toggle that uses the timing above, plus a back button or log so missed text can be reread. Show the intro once automatically on the first run and make it replayable from the menu.
- Readability: hand-lettered bubble fonts must still meet about 26 px body height at 1080p. Keep bubbles to 1-2 short lines (Vietnamese diacritics need extra line height). Never put text on parallax layers that move. Keep bubbles in screen space or locked to the panel frame.
- SFX lettering is decorative. Mirror any SFX that carries meaning ("FIRED!" stamp) in a caption, or make it readable at least briefly.

### Gaps
- No source specifically on seconds-per-panel for game comic cutscenes. The numbers above are derived from subtitle guidance.
- No user research comparing click-to-advance and auto-play for comic cutscenes was found.

## 5. Combining with paper craft / stop motion (panels as cut-outs on a desk or corkboard, sliding paper, stepped animation)

### Takeaway
Paper prototyping and comic panels go together naturally (Framed's levels were prototyped with paper cut-outs, and Comix Zone's hero literally tears through paper). The practical approach: treat each panel as a physical card with thickness, a drop shadow and slight rotation on a textured page, desk or corkboard. Animate the camera and panel moves smoothly and the content inside steppedly, and keep layered parallax inside each card for the diorama feel.

### Cited Findings
- Framed's team designed levels on paper with cut-outs and stick figures, and tuned panel movement and rotation through playtesting so it felt natural on touch. — [Game Developer: Road to the IGF, Framed](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/road-to-the-igf-loveshack-s-i-framed-i-)
- Comix Zone's hero tears through paper and jumps between panels, and panel borders burst when enemies are knocked into them. These are physical-paper metaphors for panel transitions. — [Wikipedia: Comix Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comix_Zone); [Retroware](https://articles.retroware.com/2020/11/18/comix-zone-a-farewell-to-the-genesis/)
- Gravity Rush separates the frame and panel layers, so tilting reveals depth, which is directly analogous to a layered paper diorama inside a panel. — [God is a Geek review](https://godisageek.com/2012/05/gravity-rush-review/)
- Parallax from multiple independently moving layers originates in multiplane-camera work. — [VFX Apprentice: What is 2.5D](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/what-is-2-5d-animation-games)
- Spider-Verse's halftone and Ben-Day dot texture makes frames "look printed on a page." Texture alone signals the physical medium. — [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/why-spider-man-into-the-spider-verses-comic-book-a/1100-6463955/)

### Inferences (design rules for this project)
- Stage: a warm wooden desk or kitchen counter (thematically the restaurant's counter) with a kraft-paper "page" sheet. Panels are separate cardstock cards, each 3-5 layers deep (background, mid props, character, foreground lip, frame), with a 1-2 px paper-edge highlight and a soft contact shadow.
- Entrances: cards slide in from off-page with a slight rotation (±2-4°) and settle flat, like being placed by hand. Optionally show a hand or tape piece, pinning a card with a pushpin on a corkboard for the creditor's "notice" panel, or tape the debt schedule onto the page.
- Broken frames for paper: a character cut-out pokes past the card edge (the creditor leaning into the hero's panel). The "FIRED" beat can tear the card edge, a Comix Zone homage.
- Stepped animation: inner idle loops at 8-12 fps (on twos or threes) with slight per-frame position jitter of about 0.5 px (stop-motion "boil"). Camera and parallax stay smooth at 60 fps, which keeps it readable and comfortable.
- Page transition between beats: a page flip or slide (scene-to-scene), or the camera pulls back, sweeps the finished page aside and drops the next sheet. Use the same system for win and lose endings so they feel like the final pages of the same book. The debt-schedule prop can reappear in the endings: stamped "PAID" for the win, or a red overdue stamp and the creditor's card pinned over the restaurant for the lose ending.
- Motion-sickness and comfort: keep parallax amplitude small, and provide a "reduce motion" option that disables the drift and uses fades.

### Gaps
- No developer source on paper-craft games presenting story as comic panels (for example Tearaway, Paper Mario or Bandle Tale cutscenes) was retrieved. The paper-specific rules above are inferences built from Framed, Comix Zone, Gravity Rush and general motion-comic technique.
